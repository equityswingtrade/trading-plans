The earnings wall

This is the finding that shapes everything else. The 9 October week sits in the gap between Q2 and Q3 reporting — completely clean. The 20 November contract spans almost the entire Q3 season.

There is no clean cash-secured November put in this universe

Work it through. Fifteen of the eighteen report inside the November window, which leaves CRWD, MRVL, MU. Of those three, MU needs $95,000 of cash — above the $60,000 position cap. MRVL prices at IV/RV 0.99, failing the volatility test by one hundredth. CRWD prices at 0.69, the worst ratio in the book. So the set of November cash-secured puts that are simultaneously earnings-free, affordable and fairly paid is empty.

That is why the November leg is built entirely from put credit spreads. It is not a capital-efficiency preference — it is the only structure that lets you hold a 25-delta short put through a Q3 print with a loss you have agreed to in advance. You asked for spreads where needed. November is where they are needed.

High volatility is not the same as high premium

The two loudest names in the set are the two worst sales. ARM carries the highest realised volatility here at 81.3% and pays 5.53% for 47 days — the fattest headline number on the page — at a ratio of 0.91. CrowdStrike runs 79.3% realised and pays 3.37%, at 0.69, the worst in the book. Meanwhile KLA at 43.7% realised pays 4.59% at a ratio of 1.43, and Arista at 37.9% pays 3.41% at 1.42. Restricting the screen to high-volatility names raises the premium on offer; it does nothing for the edge, and this week it inverted the two.

Nvidia, for the fourth run running, fails on price — 0.97 on November and 0.81 on the week, with the best chart (8.10) and by far the deepest chain (21,772 open interest on the November 215, a 2.2% bid/ask) in the report. Its ratio has run 0.84, 0.82, 0.82, 0.97. The gap is closing.

What a spread buys you here
The November book earns $7,702 on $22,298 of risk. The same 24 trades cash-secured would tie up $460,500 — more than the account. That is the whole argument, and on this particular book it is also the only way to express the trade.
What it costs you
No assignment, so no shares, no covered call, no recovery over months. A spread that goes wrong is finished on 20 November at its maximum loss. On a high-volatility mega-cap book that is a real and frequent outcome, not a tail.
Sizing
Weekly puts: equal-dollar targets capped at 20% per name, 95% of the account deployable. November spreads: total risk capped at 8% of the account. Weekly spreads: 4%.

When it breaks

failure mode 01

Assigned on the week — sell the call

The designed outcome on the cash-secured side, and the reason the week is cash-secured rather than spread. Each name carries its own recovery number, priced from the assignment price rather than today’s spot: Intel at a $100.13 basis writes the 110 call for about $4.90 a cycle, Palantir $7.33 on a $163.33 basis, Arista $5.46, Marvell $9.39. Never write the call below basis.

trigger  stock below the strike at 4pm Friday 9 October  →  take the shares, write the call next session at or above basis

failure mode 02

A November spread goes through the print

Fifteen of the eighteen report inside that window and all six spread lines carry one. There is nothing to manage on the night: the loss is capped at the width minus the credit and it is already paid for. What you must not do is roll a losing spread through a second print or add contracts to average down — the defined risk only stays defined if you stop adding to it.

trigger  short strike breached after the report  →  close for whatever is left, or hold to expiry. No rolls through a second event

failure mode 03

The semiconductor complex moves as one

Ten of these eighteen names are chips or chip equipment. NVDA, AMD, INTC, MU, ARM and MRVL on one side; ASML, LRCX, AMAT and KLAC on the other. They do not diversify each other in a drawdown — they are one bet on the same capex cycle. The caps hold the plans to five semi names and two per sub-sector, which is why the weekly book contains Arista, Palantir and SpaceX alongside the chips rather than six variations of the same trade.

trigger  SOX down more than 5% in a session, or two semi names through their stops  →  stop opening new semiconductor lines for the week

failure mode 04

Everything falls together

At −8% the weekly put book loses $4,861 while the November spreads are still holding their full credit. At −25% the weekly book is −$51,742, 17% of the account, and the spread book is pinned at −$22,298. The two books fail in opposite shapes, which is the main reason to run both rather than double either.

failure mode 06

You chase the volatility instead of the edge

The screen you asked for selects for movement, and movement and markup are not the same thing. The three fattest yields in this set — ARM at 5.53%, Intel at 4.64%, KLA at 4.59% — carry ratios of 0.91, 1.05 and 1.43. One is a bad sale, one is roughly fair, one is the best in the book. Nothing on the surface of those three numbers tells you which is which.

trigger  a position justified by its premium rather than its ratio  →  it does not go in the book

Data & method

  • Candidate pool: the 150 most actively traded names in the 506-name universe, used as a safe superset of $200bn+ market caps — no company of that size trades thinly enough to fall outside it. Daily bars to the 2 October 2026 close.
  • Market capitalisation from live fundamentals at the same close. 65 names carry 30-day realised volatility at or above 35%; 22 of those are $200bn or larger; 18 also clear stage 1.
  • Account $300,000; 20% single-position concentration limit, so a maximum of $60,000 of cash per name. Minimum account per name = cash ÷ 20%.
  • Strikes chosen by live delta: the 20 November leg is the listed strike closest to −0.25, the 9 October leg closest to −0.20. Where the strike grid is coarse the realised delta is shown and differs from target — MU and ASML trade on 50-point November grids.
  • Earnings dates from the market earnings calendar, 5 October to 20 November. Unverified dates are marked in each name’s detail panel.
  • Covered-call premiums are modelled (Black–Scholes, 28 DTE) priced from the assignment price rather than today’s spot, at the implied volatility quoted on each name’s live 18 December call. Pricing them from spot would overstate the recovery badly on strikes this far out of the money.
  • Volume profile: each session’s volume spread evenly across the price bands its high–low range covers, binned into 44 bands over the visible window.
