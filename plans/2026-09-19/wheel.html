Assignment is not the failure mode. Assignment below the stop is.

Amgen: the stop cost $795, and that is worth saying plainly

Capital at $300,000

The 20% concentration limit caps a single cash-secured put at $60,000, and every name in this report clears it. The binding constraint is not capital — it is how many names pass both screens with a chain you can actually trade, which is why positions are sized in multiple contracts.

cash to secure one put same trade as a credit spread

The arithmetic
Max single position $60,000. Low-volatility core $225,000 (75%), high-volatility sleeve $75,000 (25%). This week’s monthly plan deploys 96.3% across 21 contracts in 11 names, with the core sleeve exactly full at $225,000 and the high-IV sleeve at $64,000.
Where the sizing bites
Equal-dollar targets hand the most contracts to the cheapest names, which is how APA ends up with six lines — $25,500, or 8.5% of the account, in one 43%-volatility E&P name on a 1.06 ratio. It is inside every rule and it is still the position to trim first if you want a smaller book.
Spreads
The whole spread book risks $14,822 — 4.9% of the account — for $3,428 of credit, against $289,000 of cash for $5,320. More credit on a twentieth of the capital. What you give up is the wheel: no assignment, no shares, no covered call, no recovery.
Minimum account, per name
Still cash ÷ 20%. Microsoft’s $47,500 put needs $237,500; Marathon’s $39,000 needs $195,000; Valero’s $38,000 needs $190,000. All inside $300,000 — none of them inside $100,000.

When it breaks

failure mode 01

Assigned — sell the call

The designed outcome, and last week gave a live example: Occidental assigned $0.16 below the $59 weekly strike, basis $58.72, still +$12 on the credit. Each candidate carries its own number: Intel at a $96.55 basis writes the 105 call for about $14.28 a cycle (8.9% on basis), Marathon $50.05 on a $380.70 basis (6.7%), Valero $48.40 (6.7%), Coca-Cola $3.63 (2.7%). Never write the call below basis.

trigger  stock below the strike at 4pm on expiry Friday  →  take the shares, write the call next session at or above basis

failure mode 02

The strike breaks early

Roll down and out for a net credit only, twice at most. A spread rolls the same way but the long leg costs you on the way out — check the net before assuming a roll is available.

trigger  short-put delta reaches −0.50, or a close below the 60-day low in the stop table

failure mode 03

The stop fires — and sometimes costs you

Amgen is the worked example in both directions: the stop fixed a −$2,685 loss on 11 September, and the stock has since recovered above it, so holding would have marked −$1,890. The stop cost $795 in that instance. It stays, because the distribution it protects against is not symmetric — the cases where a broken name keeps falling are the ones that end a year. The largest single stop line this week is Valero at $2,742, then Marathon at $2,696 and Intel at $2,238 across two contracts.

trigger  assigned stock closes below the hard stop  →  close it. No averaging down, and no second-guessing it afterwards

failure mode 04

The sector moves as one

Marathon and Valero produced $1,568 of last week’s profit between them — more than half the total — from a single refining-margin trade. That works both ways, and at −30% the put book loses $68,297. The sector caps (two per sector, four across energy) exist for this; without them the top of the ranking this week would have been five energy names.

trigger  VIX doubles from entry, or the S&P closes below its 200-day  →  stop opening new positions of either kind

The fill never comes
