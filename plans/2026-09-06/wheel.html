What each trade actually costs

A cash-secured put ties up strike × 100 in cash. Sizing any single position at more than a fifth of the account is how one bad name takes the year, so the honest minimum account is cash ÷ 20%. Below, faded bars are the cash a put needs; solid bars are the same trade expressed as a put credit spread — buy a lower strike, cap the loss, and the capital requirement collapses.

cash-secured put, fits $100k cash-secured put, needs a bigger account same trade as a credit spread

The trade-off
A spread is not a wheel. You cannot be assigned into shares, so there is no covered-call leg, no dividend, and no way to hold and recover. You are buying a defined-risk bet that the strike holds, and you keep roughly 60–80% of the naked credit for a fraction of the capital.
When to use the put
You want the shares, the cash is available, and the name is one you would hold through a drawdown. The recovery path — assignment then covered calls — is the whole point.
When to use the spread
The name is too expensive for your account, or you want the premium without the ownership. Max loss is fixed and known before you enter, which also means it can be lost in full: a spread has no stop that helps you once the stock is through the long strike.
Sizing the spread book
Rank by return on risk, one position per sector, total max loss capped at 8% of the account. Nine spreads use $7,472 of buying power — 7.5% — against $73,550 for eight cash-secured puts.

What each book loses when everything falls together

The spread book earns more ($1,828 against $1,220) on a tenth of the capital, and in a mild selloff it loses less. But its loss stops growing only because it was capped from the start: at −20% and beyond, both books converge on their worst case, and the spread book has no shares to hold and no calls to write against them. The put book’s loss is recoverable over months; the spread book’s is final on Oct 16.

When it breaks

failure mode 01

Assigned — sell the call

Only happens on the cash-secured side, and it is the designed outcome. Each candidate carries its own number: Valero at $337.30 basis writes the 350 call for about $20.83 a cycle, Amgen $18.17, Dollar Tree $5.55, Verizon $1.27. Never write the call below basis.

trigger  stock below the strike at 4pm on expiry Friday  →  take the shares, write the call next session at or above basis

failure mode 02

The strike breaks early

Roll down and out for a net credit only, twice at most. A spread rolls the same way but the long leg costs you on the way out — check the net before assuming a roll is available.

trigger  short-put delta reaches −0.50, or a close below the 60-day low in the stop table

failure mode 03

The assigned name keeps falling

The stop table gives the level and the dollar cost per name, and how many covered-call cycles earn it back. On a spread there is nothing to decide: below the long strike the loss is fixed and the position is done.

trigger  assigned stock closes below the hard stop  →  close it. No averaging down

failure mode 04

Everything falls together

At −10% the put book loses $1,544 and the spread book $5,078. Past −20% the spread book is simply at max loss on most legs — $7,472 gone with no recovery path. Size it as if you will lose all of it in a bad quarter, because that is exactly what a defined-risk book does.

trigger  VIX doubles from entry, or the S&P closes below its 200-day  →  stop opening new positions of either kind

failure mode 05

The fill never comes

New this run because the big names are thin. Regeneron’s October 770 has five contracts of open interest and a $7.10/$14.50 market; Vertex’s 480 long leg is $1.40/$4.70 on 24. A spread quoted at mid can be 30% worse in reality, and on a two-leg order you cross two spreads, not one. Work the limit, and if it does not fill at a price that keeps the edge, skip the trade.

trigger  bid/ask wider than a third of the credit, or open interest under ~100  →  limit at mid, no chasing

Data & method

  • Universe: S&P 500 plus Nasdaq-100 additions — 480 names with a full year of daily bars. 260 cleared median daily dollar volume above $150M, price above $15 with no upper limit, above the 200-day, and a 12-month drawdown better than −55%. 28 were priced against live chains.
  • Minimum account = cash to secure the put ÷ 20%, a single-position concentration limit.
  • Volume profile: each session’s volume spread evenly across the price bands its high–low range covers, binned into 44 bands over the visible window; the point of control is the busiest band and the value area the 70% of volume around it.
  • Quotes, chains, Greeks, implied volatility, open interest and earnings dates: live pulls from the connected brokerage account, Fri 04 Sep 2026 close. Every put price, including both spread legs, is a live mark.
  • Covered-call premiums are modelled (Black–Scholes, 35 DTE) at the implied volatility quoted on each name’s live November call where one exists, otherwise at the October implied volatility.
  • Technical rules from cash-secured put timing guidance and the 20-MA pullback framework; delta and duration from the ApexVol five-year wheel backtest.
