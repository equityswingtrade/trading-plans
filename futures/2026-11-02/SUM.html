What Friday showed — and why stocks fell after earnings. Friday was the worst Nasdaq day since June: the Nasdaq Composite −2.86%, the S&P 500 −1.82%, the Dow −1.09%, closing October with the Nasdaq's worst month since March 2025 (Yahoo Finance, CNBC). The earnings did not have to be bad to sink the stocks; they had to fall short of prices that already assumed a beat:

  1. Misses at the top of the index. Alphabet missed (Thursday: its worst day on record) and Amazon missed on revenue ($201.9B) and EPS ($1.89) even with AWS +39% (Motley Fool). When the largest index weights miss, passive and index money sells the whole basket, not one stock.
  2. AI spending is eating the cash. Amazon spent about $77B in the quarter, kept its $220B plan, and showed a free-cash-flow OUTFLOW of over $42B for the twelve months; Alphabet's free cash flow was −$9.07B. The market is now asking when that spending pays, and a negative cash-flow line answers "not yet".
  3. Guidance. Intel guided Q4 revenue to $15 – 16B against $17.1B expected (PC demand hit by memory-chip prices) and fell about 29%; Reddit, Roblox and Pinterest also fell on their outlooks.
  4. Rates and oil underneath it. Two Fed presidents said rates may need to rise, December hike odds rose after Thursday's inflation data, and Brent traded about $112 – 115 on Iran-strike concerns. Higher-for-longer rates cut what a future AI payoff is worth today, which hits the most expensive (Nasdaq) stocks hardest.
  5. Gold fell too (−3.4% to about $3,885, its lowest since November 2025), on a day Treasury yields FELL — so not a real-rate move. The coverage I found does not explain it; my read is liquidation alongside equities and crypto (a $6.3B Binance hack hit crypto the same day). The only buyer that showed up in size was the month-end MOC: ES +62.25 and NQ +340.00 off the lows in the last 15 minutes.
